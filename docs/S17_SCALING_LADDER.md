# S17 — The RAW scaling ladder: capability turns immediately, and 4× compute does not save it

Experiment **X0016**, sealed at `record_hash` recorded in `labstore/X/X0016.json`, **190/190 cells
COMPLETED**, 0 INVALID. Preregistration `tools/s17/s17-preregistration.json` hash
`sha256:ef775882bd980a8fc3fd2b5212b2a2cfd527cf90873f39781a43078c5ffa41c7`. Issue
[Mnehmos/chess-vision-studio-lab#17](https://github.com/Mnehmos/chess-vision-studio-lab/issues/17),
the axis S14, S15 and S16 all pointed at.

**Answer to the preregistered question.** Under the frozen S13–S16 contract, the raw 768-input
evaluator's best static loss belongs to the **smallest** models: the curve turns at **H=2 (1,541
parameters)** and every larger rung is flat-to-worse, with H=8 and H=16 *strictly* worse. The 4×
student-compute control does **not** rescue any width — at H=128 more compute is strictly harmful,
and all three control rungs remain 3.5–4.6 milliloss worse than the 1,541-parameter model. So the
turn-up is **neither** ordinary capacity saturation **nor** undertraining at this compute envelope:
on this 18,953-row corpus the extra capacity is not merely useless, it is damaging, and more
optimizer work does not make it pay.

| seeds | 20 for H ≤ 32 (the S13–S16 list, anchors stay comparable), 10 for H ≥ 64 | 10 |

Nothing else changes: same 18,953 rows, same exam, no new labels, no teacher change, no per-width
tuning. The control's **predeclared limitation**: failure to rescue separates "capacity saturated"
from "compute helped less than 4×", not from "no compute would help".

| 4 | 3,081 | 63.1 | 0.016280 | +0.000697 | unresolved |

| 16 | 12,321 | 15.8 | 0.018735 | +0.001412 | **worse** |

- **Best rung: H=2**, 1,541 parameters.
- **Knee: H=2** — the only step with a negative marginal gain (−0.000406, unresolved).
- **Flat or worse: H = 4, 32, 64, 128, 512**; **strictly worse: H = 8 and H = 16**.
- **Marginal gain per added parameter is positive (damaging) for every step above H=2** except
  H=256, and that one is unresolved: from H=8 upward the curve adds parameters at
  +0.007…+0.45 milliloss per 1,000 parameters.
- Cost scales the wrong way too: H=512 trains in 15.8 s and serializes to 8.6 MB versus 68 KB at
  H=32, while being worse than a 1.5K-parameter model.
- Phase slices at H=512 versus H=32: endgame 0.0615 vs 0.0396, middlegame 0.0303 vs 0.0219,
  opening 0.0135 vs 0.0108 — the large model is worse in every phase, proportionally worst in the
  endgame.

These rungs also reproduce the anchors used by the earlier studies exactly (H=16: 0.018735, the
same value S15's control arm recorded; H=32: 0.019586, matching S14/S16), so the curve and the four
prior studies are one consistent body of evidence.

`rescued_widths: []`, `still_worse_widths: [128]`. **No width was rescued.** Quadrupling the
optimizer work left every large rung not merely short of its own primary rung but far behind the
1,541-parameter model — and at H=128 the extra compute made the result strictly worse, which is
what overfitting looks like when the data is the binding constraint.

## What this means

* The preregistered dichotomy — capacity-versus-compute — resolves to **neither**: capacity beyond
  ~1.5K parameters does not help this evaluator **and** 4× the compute does not make it help.
  The binding constraint is the **training signal available to the student** (18,953 rows of
  CVS-4k supervision on this population), not the student's size.
* It explains the earlier studies rather than contradicting them: S14's "RAW degrades from 3K to
  25K" was the leading edge of this whole-ladder phenomenon; S15's auxiliary gains and S14's
  representation crossover both appeared at *small* widths, where this curve says the headroom is.
* It also reframes the historical comparison: at ~197K parameters (H=256, the scale of the old raw
  NNUE) this contract produces 0.0197 — noticeably worse than 1,541 parameters. That is a
  comparison point about *this* corpus and training regime, not a verdict on the historical
  architecture, which was trained on far more data.

## Non-claims

No search, game or strength claims; static losses on one exam only. One population (18,953
positions), one teacher, one optimizer, one fixed-update regime, one machine. The control envelope
is **4×**, not unlimited compute — a null here is "4× did not help", never "nothing could". H=256's
agreement with the historical parameter scale is a comparison point, not inherited truth. The
ladder's smaller rungs have 20 seeds and the larger 10 (predeclared cost policy), so intervals are
wider above H=32.

## Next axis

#17's own follow-up trigger fires in the least convenient direction: the curve is **flat well before
H=512**, so scaling raw width is not where the static program should spend compute. The evidence
says the constraint is **data**: volume and/or quality of the supervision signal. That makes the
next static experiments the data-side ones — corpus size and the selective-labeling/coverage
question (#16) — with representation work (S14/S15) already extracted at the small widths where
this curve has its knee. No engine-search promotion is implied by anything here.
